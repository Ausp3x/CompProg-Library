## 1. Inventory names `TraversalResult: treeCycle`, but no such member or public operation exists

- **Kind:** completeness

## 9. Iterative DFS calls the asserting `g[u]` accessor twice per arc step in its innermost loop

- **Kind:** style
- **Location:** `04-Graphs/02-traversal.hpp:88`
- **Skeptics unable to refute:** 3 of 3
- **Detail:** 03-cpp.md says: "An assertion must not change an operation's asymptotic cost and must stay out of hot inner loops; check preconditions once at the entry of an operation." In `dfs`, every iteration of the frame loop evaluates `g[u].size()` (line 88) and `g[u][pos[u]++]` (line 90). `Graph::operator[]` and `CsrGraph::operator[]` (01-graph.hpp:23-24, 71-73) each run `assert(0 <= u && u < n)`, and CsrGraph also builds a span each time. That is two range asserts per arc step, under the judges' assert-enabled builds. The cost is constant-factor only, so this is low severity. bfs, connectedComponents and bipartiteCheck call `g[u]` once per vertex and are fine. Fix: look up the adjacency once per frame step, e.g. `const auto &adj = g[u];` (or `auto adj = g[u];` for span), and use `adj.size()` and `adj[pos[u]++]`. Or keep a per-frame adjacency handle on the explicit stack.
- **Repro:** Rule quote above. Code: lines 88 and 90 of 04-Graphs/02-traversal.hpp, each `g[u]` going through 01-graph.hpp:24 `assert(0 <= u && u < n)`.

## 10. relax() runs two assertions on every relaxation inside the hot loops of all algorithms

- **Kind:** style
- **Location:** `04-Graphs/05-shortest_path.hpp:39`
- **Skeptics unable to refute:** 3 of 3
- **Detail:** ShortestPathResult::relax checks endpoint ranges (line 39) and `reachable[e.from] && a >= 0` (line 40) on every call. bellmanFord (line 161), dijkstra (103), zeroOneBfs (83), dijkstraDense (122) and dagShortestPaths (141) all call relax inside their per-arc inner loops. In bellmanFord that is O(n*m) assertion evaluations, and bellmanFord already tests `out.reachable[e.from]` itself just before the call. 03-cpp.md says: "Judges run with assertions enabled. An assertion ... must stay out of hot inner loops; check preconditions once at the entry of an operation." Measured on the Bellman-Ford fixture below, the build with assertions ran about 5-10% slower than -DNDEBUG (0.165/0.174 s vs 0.150/0.148 s). The difference is noisy and does not change asymptotics, but it is exactly the placement the rule forbids. Fix: move the arc-validity checks into an unchecked internal relaxation step used by the algorithms (they already validate sources and arcs at entry), and keep the asserting public relax() only for external callers.
- **Repro:** /tmp/sprev/bench2.cpp: a 4000-vertex reversed chain with weight -1 plus 10k forward arcs, run through bellmanFord(g,0). Build with g++ -std=gnu++20 -O2, with and without -DNDEBUG, and compare times (asserting build ~0.16-0.17 s vs ~0.15 s).
