## Basic contracts

- Headers `08`–`11` common rules: sizes are below `INT_MAX`; indices and ranges follow `03-cpp.md`. Inputs are borrowed and never mutated unless a contract says so. Ties are deterministic but otherwise arbitrary. Comparators are pure strict weak orders, and equality callbacks are pure equivalences. Projections and predicates are pure and depend on element values, not addresses or positions. Callback exceptions propagate, with no rollback after mutation has begun. Each bound counts a comparison, projection, copy, move or `Count` operation as O(1).
- Omitted in the P013 re-audit sweep (2026-10-07), one reason each:
  - `randomPermutation(n)`: `iota` plus `shuffle`; sampling families are `18`.
  - Faster engine (xoshiro/xorshift): an engine swap would break replay of recorded seeds and the standard 10000th-word fixture; MT19937-64 costs a few ns per word, which no contest workload here makes the bottleneck.
  - Fast I/O fixed-length string read: `readToken` covers it; YES/NO and `bool` printing: problem-specific template material; `long double` I/O: libstdc++ falls back to platform `strtold`/`printf` on MinGW, unverifiable at the floor.
  - Compression incremental builder, neighbour queries, counting-sort build, hashed encounter ids, 2D compression: see [04-compression.md](04-compression.md) "Limits and handoffs".
  - BitOps `countOnesUpTo`, `bitReverse`; permutation `permute`, argsort, `permutationFromCycles`, `cycleType`, linear non-lexicographic rank, dynamic cycles, roots: see the omitted candidates in [06-bit_operations.md](06-bit_operations.md) and [07-permutation.md](07-permutation.md).
  - Enumeration (`12`, 2026-10-08 sweep): `forEachSupermask` adopted (the P013 handoff). Not adopted: set-bit iteration (the `x &= x - 1` idiom with `std::countr_zero`); fixed-popcount submasks (`forEachCombinationMask` over the mask's bit positions); revolving-door and cool-lex combination orders (alternative orders with no contest use beyond lexicographic); weak/strict compositions (bijection with `forEachMulticombination` by bar positions); xor-range block decomposition (a BitOps-family operation, outside P015); labeled trees (Graphs Prüfer decoding over `forEachProduct`); unlabeled trees (no standard interface); all (mask, submask) pairs (nested `forEachSubmask`, engine in `32`); a part-count cap for partitions (needs skipping, so not constant amortized in the ZS1 frame; filter by `a.size()`).
  - Knapsack (`13`, 2026-10-08 sweep): nothing adopted. Pisinger balanced subset sum `O(n * max(w))` with witness and the adaptive weight/value/meet-in-the-middle 0/1 dispatcher are leads for `29`; ordered pick sequences are compositions (`ways[w] += ways[w - w_i]` over unlimited types), not selections; fewest items for an exact weight is `KnapsackDP` with value `-1`.
  - Cycle finding (`14`, 2026-10-08 sweep): `orbitTerm` adopted. Not adopted: hash-map orbit recording, Gosper, Nivasch stack, Sedgewick–Szymanski–Yao and distinguished points, implicit orbit folds; reasons in [14-cyclefinding.md](14-cyclefinding.md).
- Sequences: approximate frequency summaries belong to `45`/`46`; `heavyHitters` in `08` is the exact Misra–Gries pass.
- P014 omissions (2026-10-08), one reason each: inversion count of every rotation and the all-range inversion table (rotation is an O(n) post-pass over `inversionCount` ranks; range inversions are Data Structures `59`); subarray-sum counting by target or divisibility (a `PrefixSum` plus hash-map composition); three/four-sum (loops over `sortedPairSum`; four-sum is meet in the middle `30`); k-machine maximum schedule and multi-machine variants (`39-scheduling.hpp`); interval jump queries (doubling over the earliest-finish successor, a query structure for Graphs `11`-style lifting, not a static batch call); circular arc cover (source not read); KACTL ConstantIntervals (Mathematics `02` search); offline set intersection (bitset technique `22`), offline RMQ (Data Structures `28`), offline range k-th (Data Structures `16`/`17` `kth`; `20` for the parallel-binary-search form), offline arithmetic-progression adds (Data Structures `05` DifferenceArray); partial sort and k-way merge (`std::partial_sort`; `44` and `15` own merging); multikey radix (chained stable `radixSort` passes).

## Advanced and Esoteric contracts

- Small-to-large (`15`) is the canonical merging/DSU-on-tree engine; Graphs `14-tree_algorithms.hpp` supplies subtree layouts and must not duplicate container merging. Amortized bounds include callback costs; ownership of merged containers is explicit.
- Hilbert order (`16`) is usable by Data Structures `23-range_query_offline.hpp` without requiring Mo; coordinate width and tie order are deterministic.
- Advanced I/O (`17`) falls back to `03` on pipes and interactive judges; input lifetime and portability are documented.
- Randomized algorithms (`18`) and hash families (`19`) state Las Vegas versus Monte Carlo guarantees, reproducible seeds and collision accounting.
- Parallel binary search (`20`) returns the first satisfying time per query, or absent; it requires a monotone check and either a reset-and-replay or rollback update model; CDQ (`21`) calls `contribute(left, right)` per split and requires a strict tie order and merge invariants; 2D offline rectangle sums belong to Data Structures `33`.
- Bitset optimization (`22`) reuses Core `18-bitset` or `std::bitset` for storage and shifts; scalar contest code only, no second dynamic-bitset engine.
- Allocator (`23`; `deallocate` is a no-op), memoization (`24`), rollback (`42`) and persistent arena (`43`) document reset semantics, alignment, object destruction, state lifetime, stack depth and version lifetime; side effects must be recorded in the undo log before mutation.
- Exact cover (`25`) enumerates bounded solutions with secondary columns; SAT (`26`) stays optional behind a concrete contest use case and a proof/testing plan, and its `hornSat` runs in linear time; 2-SAT is Graphs `18-twosat.hpp`.
- Calendar (`27`) uses the proleptic Gregorian calendar and timezone-free day numbers; encoding (`28`) rejects overlong UTF-8, surrogates and code points above `0x10FFFF`.
- Advanced knapsack (`29`) owns value/count/witness variants, grouped, two-resource and `O(n·W)` tree (dependency) knapsack, k-best and optimal-selection counts. Its optimization tables are exact-weight with `KNAPSACK_UNREACHABLE`, while `kthBestKnapsack` and `countOptimalSelections` count each selection of weight at most `w` once. The 0/1 sharp-P subset sum is Mathematics `12` `subsetSumCount`; `knapsackCountsFps` adds bounded and unlimited multiplicities under the same prime-modulus condition and is never conflated with Boolean reachability. Boolean bitset reachability is `22`; meet in the middle is `30`.
- Meet in the middle (`30`): duplicate multiplicities are counted exactly; Pareto pruning only when justified; the four-list split documents its memory tradeoff.
- Digit DP (`31`): memoization keys include every state (position, tight, leading zero, automaton state); signed intervals are handled by an explicit policy. Subset DP (`32`) documents `O(3^n)` submask costs and reconstruction; Graphs `73` owns the Hamiltonian/TSP API and depends on `32`, never the reverse. Profile DP (`33`) states canonical frontier labels, boundary closure and state invariants.
- State-space search (`35`) distinguishes exact exhaustion from budget-stopped outcomes and requires admissible lower bounds for IDA*; explicit-graph engines remain in Graphs (`43-implicit_graph.hpp` owns implicit BFS/Dijkstra). Game search (`36`) distinguishes solved values from heuristic horizons and documents depth/window semantics.
- Expression parsing (`37`) reports exact error offsets, supports configurable evaluation domains and never evaluates arbitrary code. Heuristic optimization (`38`) gives no optimality guarantee; seeds replay deterministically and incumbents are retained.
- Scheduling (`39`) records the exchange-argument assumptions of each ordering rule (Livshits–Kladov limits them to linear, exponential and identical monotone penalties); weighted interval scheduling reuses `09`, flow formulations reuse Graphs; rooted-tree precedence orders are Graphs `79`.
- Optimal merge (`40`) handles zero weights, the one-symbol case and ties, using the Data Structures heap engine; alphabetic (ordered) constraints are `48`, whose claimed `O(n log n)` Garsia–Wachs bound must be verified independently of the OI Wiki wording.
- External memory (`44`) covers cache-aware/offline algorithms for unusually large input; external sorting is the only sorting variant outside `11`. Sketches (`45`) and streaming summaries (`46`) state error, failure probability, memory and adversarial-stream contracts (`slidingWindowDistinct` adapts `45`'s HyperLogLog, `StreamReservoir` adapts `18`); mergeability is claimed only for KLL/HLL/Count-Min, not GK. Derandomization (`47`) distinguishes deterministic constructions, Monte Carlo repetition and parameterized costs.
- `52` interval DP reuses the Mathematics `27` Knuth adapter for optimal BST; `53` bracket sequences order lexicographically with `(` before `)` and reuse Mathematics `05` Catalan numbers; `54` k-best enumerations are charged `O(k log k)` with explicit tie order, and `kLargest` is the `std::greater<>` comparator argument of every `kSmallest*`; `55` Dinkelbach takes an oracle maximizing `sum(a - t*b)` and terminates exactly on integer weights by comparing rationals in `lll`.

## P075 package record (MI23)

Omitted in the 2026-10-13 sweep, one reason each:

- Meldable-heap `merge` (OI Wiki, USACO Guide): binary heaps cannot meld, and a leftist heap only merges one-sided functions; treap union gives the same `O(n log n)` total over a merge tree for two-sided functions.
- `restrictDomain`, `clampSlope` and `minPlusConvolution` on `SlopeTrick`: they delete or re-rank the far end of a binary heap. `WeightedSlopeTrick` provides them.
- `fromPoints`, `segmentFunc` (MAS): `addLinear` plus one weighted `addXMinusA` per breakpoint.
- `convolveSegment(s, w)` (MAS super): exactly `addLinear(-s)`, `slide(0, w)`, `addLinear(s)` on `WeightedSlopeTrick`.
- `sumAll` (MAS super): repeated `merge`.
- `breakpoints`/`lines` iteration (SUI): solution recovery uses `argminRange` with backward clamping; `SlopeTrick` exposes `left`, `right`, `addl`, `addr`.
- `slopeAt(x)`: `evaluate(x + 1) - evaluate(x)` on the integer domain.
- `clear` (NYA): assign a default-constructed object.
- Floating-point `WeightedSlopeTrick`: splitting real weights at a real `wl` is ill-conditioned under rounding. `SlopeTrick<double>` covers real positions with unit slopes.
- `DualSlopeTrick::evaluate` and `min`: conjugate evaluation costs `O(n)`. Use `shift` with `atZero`, or `WeightedSlopeTrick`.
- `DualSlopeTrick::addAbs`: `addXMinusA` plus `addAMinusX`, same cost. `DualSlopeTrick::restrictDomain`: it deletes the extreme slopes, the far ends of the binary heaps. `DualSlopeTrick::merge`: the conjugate of a sum is the infimal convolution of the conjugates, which binary heaps cannot form. `WeightedSlopeTrick` provides all three.
- TKO negative-argument sliding maximum: not convexity-preserving.
