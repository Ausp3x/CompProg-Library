# 05 Mathematics — inventory and research contract

Ordinary scalar mathematical algorithms live here; full Core types own applicable hardware acceleration. Basic/Advanced/Esoteric classify importance/rarity and never mix inside one header. **Existing-unverified** marks an active header only; all newly listed operations are **planned** until individually implemented and verified. **Legacy reference** means archived code, not an active implementation. P012 verifies the six MA01/MA02 foundation headers; see [package evidence](96-p012.md). Other entries retain their individual status.

Core owns reduction/modint/big-integer/matrix/polynomial types. Core Poly deliberately contains its own optimized transforms. Scalar transform/FPS/linear-algebra headers here are independently useful contest implementations; duplication is intentional and dependencies remain acyclic. Domains below are part of the contract: finite fields, composite residue rings, integers/rationals and approximate reals are not interchangeable.

## Ownership, completeness and evidence

- Every new feature above remains planned, even in a row with an existing header. Existing `FastConv`, sieve and combinatorics names identify actual migrated scope; they do not establish compliance with additions.
- Integer roots live in `integer_roots`; `numerical_methods` may call them. Prime-field roots/logs are Advanced, general prime-power/composite roots and large-order discrete-log techniques are separate Esoteric families. Fast composition/factorization and multivariate/polynomial-matrix research likewise stay in their own Esoteric headers rather than mixing tiers into ordinary polynomial algorithms.
- Core Poly/Matrix may independently implement accelerated operations from these families. Mathematics uses scalar contest implementations or the allowed Core interfaces, with no handwritten ISA kernels here. Graph counting owns graph-facing applications and witnesses; matrix-tree/Pfaffian/permanent primitives live here.
- All operations specify integer widths/overflow, modulus prime/unit assumptions, characteristic, coefficient bounds, empty/zero/singular cases and legitimate failure. Randomized bounds distinguish expected time, Monte Carlo error and verifiable Las Vegas retry. Approximate methods specify tolerance/error and conditioning; they cannot silently replace exact variants.
- Check small signed domains by exhaustive references; factor products/primality, equation substitution, CRT residuals, convolution versus `O(n * m)`, interpolation reconstruction, normal-form identities, root intervals and algebraic invariants. Tests on source libraries are inspiration, not copied evidence.
- Old mixed `05-primesandfactors.hpp`, `06-phiandinverse.hpp`, `07-chiremthmandgarner.hpp`, `08-modlogandmodroot.hpp`, `13-numericalmethods.hpp` and `14-miscellaneous.hpp` stay in OLD until their features are accounted for. Old `09-montgomery.hpp` is a Core reference; old poly/moly are references for Core Poly. No archive deletion or implementation changes are part of this inventory pass.

The 2026-09-27 audit compares external algorithm catalogs/documentation and the saved Modern Computer Arithmetic contents; precise read scope and URLs are recorded in the [central audit](../00-Guidelines/16-inventory-audit.md). This inventory aims at deliberate broad coverage, including research curiosities, and cannot certify that no future algorithm/paper exists. A selected implementation batch must refine its own API/variant checklist before coding and record discovered gaps rather than declaring unsupported completeness.

The follow-up scope pass compared the active arithmetic/search/equation/sieve/combinatorics/transform interfaces and the archived factorization/inverse/CRT/log/root/numerical helpers, plus the saved [KACTL notebook](../95-Resources/05-kactl.pdf) `Tridiagonal.h` contract. It made structured solves and legacy parameter/range variants explicit under their existing owners; existing-unverified status is unchanged.
