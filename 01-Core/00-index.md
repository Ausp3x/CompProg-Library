# 01 Core — inventory and contract

Core is the self-contained foundation for GNU C++20 contest code. Preserve the approved order `01`–`18`; append a new foundational type only for a demonstrated shared need. Core does not use Basic/Advanced/Esoteric ordering. Full and mini implementations live in separate headers with identical semantics on their shared domain; each modular mini struct is independently copyable. `Basic eligible` selects the compact aggregate/notebook profile, not a performance tier.

**Existing-unverified** means a header exists, not that every item in its row is implemented. All additions in this audit are **planned** until implementation and evidence establish otherwise. **Legacy reference** means archived source only. Verified rows link their package evidence; all other rows retain their stated status. The feature checklist is the current family worklist; detailed contracts and tests must track individual completed and outstanding features.

## Representation and scalar contracts

- Reduction: define exact modulus and dividend ranges, canonical versus lazy residues, wide intermediate bounds, zero/modulus-one handling, aliasing and transform boundaries. Barrett applies beyond odd moduli; Montgomery requires `gcd(mod, 2) = 1`. Never infer full unsigned coverage merely from the storage width.
- Modular types: normalized negative/min-signed construction, safe raw construction, ordering meaning, division by nonunits, negative exponents and batch inverse all need explicit contracts. Static primality knowledge and runtime primality assumptions differ. Modulus changes invalidate values/caches under a documented rule; IDs must remain independent.
- Rational: denominator positive and reduced, unique zero, cancellation before multiplication and exact overflow/failure rules; compare without overflowing cross-products. Ordinary rational values exclude NaN/infinity. Do not couple this rule to InfInt's separate legacy sentinel behavior.
- InfInt audit: both full and mini code contain `is_inf`, signed infinity parsing/arithmetic and truncating native conversions; full InfInt also has exact-bit-length random sampling. Preserve these as visible migration decisions: define supported sentinel operations/no-answer cases and conversion overflow behavior, or explicitly account for a replacement before removal. Finite integer semantics must remain unambiguous.
- Full integer operations: import/export limbs and bases, checked native conversion, decimal/hex/general-base parse/print, signed div/rem (state truncation versus Euclidean), exact division, gcd/xgcd/lcm, modular inverse/power, kth root with remainder/perfect-power test, two's-complement bitwise semantics, shifts, bit length/scan/popcount. Sampling needs a reproducible caller-supplied seed and distinct bounded versus exact-bit-length contracts.
- Full integer backend worklist: schoolbook/Comba, dedicated squaring, Karatsuba, Toom–Cook, balanced/unbalanced and truncated/middle products, FFT/NTT with coefficient/carry bounds; word division, normalized long division, divide-and-conquer/Burnikel–Ziegler, reciprocal/Newton, exact/Hensel and quotient/remainder-only paths; binary/Lehmer/half-GCD and fast base conversion. Select implementations and measured crossovers for supported workloads. Schönhage–Strassen, Fürer and Harvey–van der Hoeven belong to the explicitly tracked research comparison, not an unsubstantiated claim that asymptotic machinery is practically fastest.

## Matrix and bitset domains

- Common matrix operations: construction and empty rectangular shapes, identity, access, transpose/adjoint, slicing/concatenation, scalar/vector/matrix products, dot/cross on valid vector dimensions, trace, tensor/Kronecker product, powers and power sums. State operation requirements individually: semiring products need no division; field elimination does; composite residue rings require unit pivots or ring-safe methods.
- Exact dense algebra: rank/RREF, determinant, inverse, one/all solutions and nullspace, multiple right-hand sides, row/column-space intersection, PLU/PLUQ, adjugate including singular cases, characteristic/minimal polynomial, Krylov/Frobenius and Jordan forms (with required splitting-field support) and polynomial-in-matrix evaluation. Fraction-free/Bareiss, CRT reconstruction and division-free Berkowitz cover integer/ring cases. Structured Toeplitz/Hankel/Vandermonde, banded/tridiagonal (including cyclic) solves and rank-one updates are explicit extensions, with unit-pivot conditions and generic fallback.
- Specialized counts: preserve and audit the existing full Matrix `hafnian` method; permanent/Pfaffian/Hafnian/loop-Hafnian are tracked full-type extensions with explicit characteristic assumptions. The ordinary scalar research APIs live in Mathematics `combinatorial_linear_algebra`; intentional full-Core duplication remains allowed.
- Approximate dense algebra: separately named/API-scoped real/complex solves, pivoting/rank policy, LU, QR/least squares, Cholesky for positive-definite symmetric/Hermitian inputs and pivoted LDL for its stated indefinite domain, SVD/pseudoinverse and eigen/Schur research extensions. State conditioning, residual/error criteria and nonconvergence; finite-field rank is not an epsilon test. Complex conjugation is distinct from transpose. Only add numerical extensions with defensible stability evidence.
- GF(2): packed layouts, padding bits, rectangular multiplication, Four Russians/M4RI crossover, row/column spaces and intersection, all-solution witnesses and word-boundary cases. Boolean OR-AND reachability multiplication is a separately named semiring operation, not GF(2) multiplication.
- Sparse: sorted indices/duplicates/explicit zeros, rectangular/empty shapes, transpose and conversions, SpMV/SpMM, dense crossover and fill-in. Wiedemann/block Wiedemann, Lanczos, random preconditioning, sparse rank/determinant/solve/minimal polynomial and operator powers need field-size assumptions and Monte Carlo versus Las Vegas guarantees. Numerical CG requires SPD/Hermitian PD; GMRES/BiCGSTAB and preconditioners need separate convergence/breakdown contracts. The existing `steadyState` performs a fixed number of matrix-vector products and does not test convergence: retain that operation with an honest name/contract, and separately specify stationary-state iteration with residual, convergence and periodic/reducible-chain behavior; Mathematics owns the Markov-chain model.
- Bitset is a shared primitive for BitMatrix, subset DP and graph search. Define size mismatch, padding, shifts at/above length, tail masking, iterator invalidation and mutation aliasing. Dynamic succinct rank/select or compressed bitmap indexes belong in Data Structures; `std::bitset` remains the compact fixed-size alternative. Avoid a dependency cycle: the generic bitset must not depend on BitMatrix.

## Polynomial and FPS domains

- Representation: coefficient order, zero normalization versus retained FPS precision, truncation, ring/field/characteristic constraints and sparse/dense crossover. Integers/rationals, prime fields, composite residues and approximate real/complex coefficients have distinct supported operation sets.
- Convolution: schoolbook/Karatsuba, FFT with a quantified exactness envelope, NTT and arbitrary-mod CRT, signed integer reconstruction, cyclic/negacyclic, truncated/middle products, squaring, sparse products and multivariate packing; mixed-radix/Bluestein/Rader transforms when useful, modulo `2^64` and binary-extension-field convolution as separate exact domains; precompute/cache lifetime and maximum transform length. Core owns acceleration even when ordinary Mathematics variants intentionally duplicate functionality.
- Algebra: add/multiply/div-rem, monic and pseudo-division where applicable, modular polynomial power/inverse, gcd/xgcd/half-GCD, resultant/subresultants/discriminant, square-free decomposition and finite-field factorization/roots. Integer/rational factorization and certified numerical roots are separate domain paths; Mathematics owns the broader scalar/reference research families.
- FPS: derivative/integral, inverse, log/exp, integer/rational/general powers under stated unit/characteristic conditions, sqrt/kth roots with valuation and branch choices, trigonometric/hyperbolic/inverse series when valid, ordinary/exponential generating-function conversions and linear differential-equation solvers. Characteristic-induced nonexistence or nonuniqueness must be visible.
- Evaluation/interpolation: Horner and divide-and-conquer products, subproduct/remainder trees, arbitrary/equidistant/geometric/chirp-z points, Hermite repeated-point jets, Taylor shift, factorial/Newton basis conversions and transposed operations; repeated points require Hermite data or explicit rejection.
- Advanced engine: modular/ordinary composition (Brent–Kung and researched faster alternatives), reversion, power projection, bivariate coefficient extraction, rational-series coefficients/Bostan–Mori, Fiduccia/recurrences, Padé/rational interpolation, partial-fraction decomposition and online/relaxed/semi-relaxed multiplication; fast arithmetic/geometric polynomial product sequences, sparse FPS and change-of-basis variants. General multivariate Gröbner systems, polynomial matrices and specialized combinatorial matrix counts remain visible Mathematics families, not hidden promises of a single univariate type.

## Evidence and research boundary

Each header must compile when included alone via its declared direct dependencies. Document public operation time/memory, domains and legitimate failures. Full profiles require applicable compile-time acceleration plus scalar fallback and measured thresholds; no backend may silently change arithmetic semantics. Differential-check full/mini and independent exact references, empty/zero/singular cases, composite moduli, overflow limits, aliasing, threshold neighbors and scalar/accelerated paths.

The 2026-09-27 inventory audit compared current Core declarations/selected bodies, Modern Computer Arithmetic's contents and the external catalog/documentation sources recorded in the [central audit](../00-Guidelines/16-inventory-audit.md). Catalog inspection establishes missing feature families, not implementation correctness or a claim of surveying every paper. Newly listed extensions remain planned, including extensions inside existing-unverified rows. Revisit the inventory when a selected family's detailed research reveals further cases.

The follow-up scope pass inspected the current Matrix/BitMatrix/SparseMatrix and archived Poly/Moly public operations, full/mini InfInt conversion and sentinel interfaces, and the saved [KACTL notebook](../95-Resources/05-kactl.pdf) tridiagonal-solver contract. It made structured solves and the Mini/SparseMatrix migration obligations explicit; it did not verify their implementations.
