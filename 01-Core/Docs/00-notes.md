# 01 Core — Notes

Folder-wide contracts, numeric policies and cross-folder ownership. Operation lists live only in [00-index.md](../00-index.md), sources in [00-sources.md](00-sources.md), and the meaning of a row's `required:` groups in `00-Guidelines/02-structure.md` (Inventory Contract).

## Ownership Boundaries

- Integer roots on native words (isqrt, icbrt, kth root with remainder, perfect-power tests) are Mathematics `21-integer_roots.hpp`; overflow-checked powers and checked or saturating add and mul are Mathematics `01-mod_arithmetic.hpp`. InfInt keeps its own big-integer roots.
- Primitive roots, discrete logarithms, composite-modulus roots, CRT and finite-field extensions are Mathematics families; a residue type does not claim them.
- Markov-chain models and stationary-distribution theory are Mathematics `26-probability.hpp`; SparseMatrix provides only the iteration primitives of its row.
- Succinct and dynamic rank/select bit vectors are Data Structures `40`/`49`; `std::bitset` stays the compact fixed-size alternative.
- The incremental XOR linear basis and its prefix and range variants are Data Structures `62-xorbasis.hpp`; BitMatrix owns packed GF(2) matrices, elimination and subspace operations.

## Reduction and Modular Types

- Each reducer and modular type defines its exact modulus and dividend ranges, canonical versus lazy residues, wide intermediate bounds, zero and modulus-one handling, aliasing and transform boundaries. Barrett applies to every nonzero word modulus, even ones included; Montgomery requires an odd modulus. Full unsigned coverage is never inferred from the storage width alone.
- Modular types state normalized construction from negative and minimum signed values, safe raw construction, the meaning of ordering (canonical representatives), division by nonunits, negative exponents and batch inverse. Static primality knowledge differs from a runtime primality assumption (the `prm` hint of `setMod`). Every modulus change invalidates values and caches under the documented rule; IDs and widths stay independent.
- Modulus one is the zero ring: zero is the identity and a unit, and prime-root APIs are unavailable there.
- Left out of Barrett and Montgomery, with reasons:
  - `div` and `multiplier`: wrappers of `divMod(x).first` and `Multiplier(r, b)`.
  - A divisibility test: it saves one multiply over `reduce(x) == 0`, needs a ceiling reciprocal and a narrower dividend, and no contest catalog has it.
  - Signed-dividend reduction: the signed constructors of `05-modint.hpp` own normalization.
  - A Shoup quotient: `NTL MulDivRem` is deprecated upstream.
  - Barrett `add`/`sub`/`neg`: trivial, and owned by the modular types.
  - `pow` with a fixed factor: squaring gains nothing from it.
  - Lazy Barrett outputs: the correction is one compare-subtract on an independent product, and no chained lazy domain exists for ordinary words.
  - A 128-bit dividend for Barrett32 and an array `reduce`: no catalog has them; fold the high word or use Barrett64.
  - Lemire's 32-bit direct remainder: it takes only 32-bit dividends and the same two multiplies.
  - Montgomery `neg`, `one`, lazy equality and `square`: `sub(0, a)`, `init(1)`, a `normalize` comparison, and no squaring gain at word size.
  - Residue inverses and batched powers: `05-modint.hpp` owns `inv`, the Montgomery power threshold and Miller–Rabin.
  - 64-bit, AVX-512/IFMA and signed-Montgomery lanes: AVX2 has no 64x64 multiply, the ISA set is AVX2/FMA/BMI2, and the signed form is a Poly NTT kernel decision.
- The minis lack, until P255 brings them to parity with the full type (`00-Guidelines/01-principles.md`, Profiles): `MontModInt64`, `DynMontModInt64`, `sqrt`/`trySqrt`, primality flags and detection, prime roots, batch inverse, decimal streams, integer casts, truthiness, ordering and increment/decrement; `val()` passes residues outward. `ModInt61` gets a mini alias (`ModInt61Mini`); its Mersenne reduction is a full-type internal. `!=` is rewritten from `==` in C++20, and mixed integer operands convert through the constructor.
- Left out of the modular types, with reasons:
  - Extra aliases such as `modint1000000007`/`mint107`: one alias suffices, and `ModInt<1000000007>` is one token longer.
  - `umod()`: `mod()` already returns the unsigned word.
  - A `randgen` or random hash base on `ModInt61`: Strings `randomBase` owns it.
  - FPS constant-term hooks `log(mint)`/`exp(mint)`: Poly conventions, not residue arithmetic.
  - `ntt_info`, `can_ntt` and primitive-root descriptors: the Poly root cache and Mathematics own primitive roots.
  - `std::hash`, `to_string` and a `balanced()` signed representative: no catalog or no demonstrated need.
  - `norm`: the constructors' internal step, not an operation of the row (user decision 2026-11-21).
- The backend candidates of the catalog sweeps, among them a Goldilocks-specialized reduction (ecnerwala `mod_goldilocks`) and a binary-GCD inverse with a table of small inverses (Nyaan `fast_modinv`), carry measured verdicts in [05-modint.md](05-modint.md#optimization-candidates).
- Stream extraction of a residue reads one signed decimal token as `cin >> int` does: a leading sign and every digit belong to the token whatever the stream's locale classes as white space, and white space itself follows the locale (user decision 2026-11-21).
- Arithmetic modulo 2^64 is native `ulng` wraparound; no dedicated residue type was adopted, and the odd-unit inverse is Mathematics `01-mod_arithmetic.hpp` `invMod2p64`.

## Reduction Family

- `03-barrett.hpp` and `04-montgomery.hpp` (P004) specify machine-word arithmetic only. `05-modint.hpp` owns modular integer types, primality and residue inverses; multiword truncated or folded Barrett, Montgomery–Svoboda, FastREDC and McLaughlin multiplication belong to `07-infint.hpp` and `16-poly.hpp` (MCA §§2.4.1–2.4.4 and §2.5, [00-sources.md](00-sources.md#previous-source-keys-and-audit-narrative)).
- Backend choice: Barrett for even moduli, independent products and arbitrary wide dividends (no representation conversion); Montgomery for an odd modulus with many chained products (convert once, convert back); `Barrett32::Multiplier` or `Barrett64::Multiplier` for a repeated factor. Modulus one returns zero throughout, exponent zero included.
- The modular types add one reducer of their own, `modint_detail::Reciprocal` (`05-modint.hpp`): the Möller–Granlund 2-by-1 division for products of canonical residues of 64-bit moduli, with one precomputed word and no representation conversion. It repeats the step that `infint_detail::invWord` and `div2by1` take on InfInt's limbs (`07-infint.hpp`) instead of sharing it, because Core order puts 05 before 07, InfInt includes only the template, and each copy is a few lines inside its own detail namespace (user decision 2026-11-17).
- Special-prime, floating-point quotient, IFMA/AVX-512, GPU and residue-number-system variants need separate domain proofs and measured workloads. No floating-point exactness or constant-time behaviour is promised. Benchmarks: [03-barrett.md](03-barrett.md#benchmarks), [04-montgomery.md](04-montgomery.md#benchmarks).

## Matrices

- Operation requirements are stated individually: semiring products need no division, field elimination does, and composite residue rings require unit pivots or ring-safe methods (Bareiss, CRT, Berkowitz). Finite-field rank is not an epsilon test.
- Full Matrix derives moduli through `T::mod()` with correct word widths and replaces static field constraints with a runtime contract for dynamic types ([C03 handoff](05-modint.md#dependent-owner-handoff)). Matrices and cached factors are rebuilt after a dynamic modulus change.
- Approximate algebra is separately named and scoped: it states its pivoting and rank tolerance, conditioning, residual and error criteria, and nonconvergence. Cholesky requires positive-definite symmetric or Hermitian input, pivoted LDL its stated indefinite domain. Complex conjugation is distinct from transpose. Numerical extensions are added only with defensible stability evidence.
- Structured solves (Toeplitz, Hankel, Vandermonde, banded and tridiagonal including cyclic, rank-one updates) state unit-pivot conditions and a generic fallback. The existing `hafnian` is preserved and audited; permanent, Pfaffian, Hafnian and loop Hafnian carry explicit characteristic assumptions, and Mathematics `48-combinatorial_linear_algebra.hpp` owns the scalar research APIs (duplication allowed).
- GF(2) contracts cover packed layouts, padding bits, rectangular multiplication, the Four Russians crossover, row and column spaces and their intersection, all-solution witnesses and word-boundary cases. Boolean OR-AND reachability multiplication is a separately named semiring operation, never GF(2) multiplication.
- Sparse contracts cover sorted indices, duplicates and explicit zeros, rectangular and empty shapes, transpose and conversions, the dense crossover and fill-in. Wiedemann, block Wiedemann, Lanczos and random preconditioning state field-size assumptions and Monte Carlo versus Las Vegas guarantees. `steadyState` performs a fixed number of products and tests no convergence; it keeps an honest contract, and `stationaryState` is specified separately with residual, convergence and periodic or reducible chain behaviour. CG requires SPD or Hermitian PD input; GMRES, BiCGSTAB and the preconditioners need separate convergence and breakdown contracts.
- Mini matrices, until P021, P022 and P024 bring them to parity (`00-Guidelines/01-principles.md`, Profiles): `MatrixMini` works over stated fields only; `BitMatrixMini` provides elimination, rank, consistency, one solution and a nullspace basis; `SparseMatrixMini` keeps dimensions, triplet ingestion with duplicate and zero rules, the vector product and repeated-product powers.

## Polynomials and FPS

- Representation: coefficient order, zero normalization versus retained FPS precision, truncation, ring, field and characteristic constraints, and the sparse/dense crossover. Integers and rationals, prime fields, composite residues and approximate coefficients have distinct supported operation sets; nonexistence or nonuniqueness caused by the characteristic is visible (exp, log, sqrt and kth-root conditions, valuation and branch choices).
- Convolution: FFT with a quantified exactness envelope; NTT with a documented root-cache lifetime and maximum transform length; arbitrary-mod CRT with signed reconstruction. Convolution modulo 2^64 and over binary extension fields are separate exact domains, duplicated as scalar Mathematics `65-convolution_specialized.hpp`.
- Evaluation and interpolation: repeated points require Hermite data or explicit rejection. Composition names its algorithm (Brent–Kung baseline, Kinoshita–Li as the faster alternative) and reversion its method (Lagrange inversion via power projection).
- General multivariate systems, Gröbner bases, polynomial matrices, set power series and specialized combinatorial counts remain Mathematics families, not promises of the univariate type.
- `16-poly.hpp` follows the type-header rule (`00-Guidelines/03-cpp.md`, API Contracts): `exp(f, n)`, `Poly<T>::interpolate`, `PolyCompanion::convolutionLng`.
- `16-poly.hpp` left out, with reasons: `remove`/`inflate`/`deflate` (index loops, no algorithm); the Möbius special composition (the same `O(n log^2 n)` bound through `compose`); modular `MinPolyMod`/`TraceMod`/`ProjectPowers` (Mathematics `42-finite_fields.hpp` owns extension-field minimal polynomials, and the transposed modular composition has no contest use); orthogonal, Fibonacci and q-series polynomials (FLINT only); online FPS inverse, exp, log and pow (online `sqrt` has no owner), composed sum and product, subset-sum products, Schönhage–Strassen and a Goldilocks-specific NTT (Mathematics `52`, `46`, `12` and `65` own them, and the generic `Ntt<T>` handles any 64-bit NTT prime through the scalar path).
- `17-polymini.hpp` uses the same NTT roots and bit-reversed order as `Poly<T>::ntt`, so every shared operation returns the same coefficients and sizes ([17-polymini.md](17-polymini.md)). Its row names Berlekamp–Massey `findLinearRecurrence`, the full type's name, so code moves between the two unchanged.
- PolyMini gaps until P257 closes them (parity, `00-Guidelines/01-principles.md`, Profiles; the reasons date from the 2026-10-10 sweep): `sqrt` (for `a[0] = 1` it is `exp(log(a, n) * (T(1) / 2), n)`; the general constant term needs a modular square root the mini does not carry); `powMod` and `x^k mod f` (`linearRecurrenceKth` covers the contest use); chirp-z; `gcd`/`resultant`; composition and its inverse; sample-point shifts; sparse series; shifts and truncation helpers (vector operations); `T * a` with the scalar first, unary minus and compound assignment (`a * c`, `a - b` and `a = a * b` say the same); arbitrary-mod convolution and Lagrange point evaluation (full `Poly` and Mathematics own them).

## Bitset

- Bitset is the shared primitive for BitMatrix, subset DP and graph search. Its contract defines size mismatch, padding, shifts at or above the length, tail masking, proxy invalidation and mutation aliasing. It does not depend on BitMatrix (no dependency cycle).
- Not adopted from the 2026-10-06 sweep: `join`/append (resize plus `combineRange`), `reversed`, index collection and `forEach` visitors (`findNext` loops), intersection visitors (`countAnd`/`&`), prefix parity, a checked `toUllong` (`blocks()[0]`), `isProperSubsetOf`, `lowbit` (`findFirst`), lexicographic ordering and hashing (exclusions documented in [18-bitset.md](18-bitset.md)).

## Fast I/O

- Core owns stream I/O: `FastInput`/`FastOutput` in [19-fastio.md](19-fastio.md), moved from `06-Miscellaneous/03-fastio.hpp` on 2026-11-01, absorbing planned row 17. `06-Miscellaneous/50-interactive.hpp` keeps its own judge protocol type.
